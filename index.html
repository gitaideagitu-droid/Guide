What is Generative AI?

Knowing the Depths of Generative AI Ocean!

In terms of functionality, Generative AI is a multimodal AI system that assists industries in reducing time spent on generating text, realistic images, and short videos; summarizing long texts, and helping in complex tasks like identifying defects in designs.

 

So, what is Generative AI?

Generative AI is an artificial intelligence system that uses deep learning models to generate content taking cues from the data it is trained on. The data can be in any form, such as images or human-readable text. The term Generative AI is coined as it can generate content – whether text, audio, or images. It performs such actions by detecting the underlying patterns of the input. The patterns act as a basis for creating similar content that is so realistic that it becomes very difficult to differentiate it from human-created works.

To make things much simpler, Generative AI is the creative side of AI that processes large sets of data and creates new content such as images, content, music, and more.

 

The Evolution of Generative AI

In the 1950s, when scientists introduced the concept of Machine Learning (ML) to the world, they were also toying with the idea of using Machine Learning to generate new data. Markov Chain is one of the first few examples of Generative AI which could be used to generate sequences of data based on the inputs. Unfortunately, the computational power and data resources didn’t support the learning activity in those days. Nevertheless, the technological developments and availability of digital data helped in thrusting the machine learning curve forward between the years 1990 – 2000.

  • Technically, Generative AI was performing minimal functions, such as chatbot back in 1966.
  • In the proceeding years, experts working on AI turned these simple chatbots into highly capable tools.
  • After publishing the white paper “Attention is All You Need” in 2015, and performing thorough research for 4 years, in 2018, scientists introduced the concept of ‘Transformers.”
  • Transformers replaced many old algorithms that were complex and were not able to generate superior quality output.
  • OpenAI introduced ChatGPT in the year 2020 which uses GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) models on neural networks using deep learning architecture to generate content.
  • OpenAI brought another tool DALL-E in 2021 with the ability to generate realistic images based on the command prompts using text.
  • A few eminent tech companies are racing to introduce Generative AI to new realms of work to reduce the burden on humans.

How Does Generative AI Work?

Crafting the Formula for Creative AI!

Generative AI can learn the patterns and structures from the given data sets and then apply the knowledge in creating new data that is similar to the original dataset. Let’s get into understanding how this formula works.

 

Training Data

The initial training of Generative AI starts by sharing enormous amounts of datasets of the type of data that is required to be generated. For instance, if the Generative AI is intended to generate human face images, the datasets fed for training also consist of a large collection of human face images.

 

Model Architecture

In Generative AI, complex neural network architectures such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) or Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) are used to learn the underlying patterns and features that are present in the given training data. These architectures boost the performance of the model.

 

Learning Patterns

As a next step in the training process, the model learns to identify patterns and correlations within the available datasets. For instance, Generative AI will learn to identify common facial features like eyes, nose, and mouth from the given human faces’ datasets.

 

Generating New Data

Once the training is completed, the Generative AI model produces new data samples based on the learned ones. By providing input data, we can test Generative AI by allowing it to produce similar output data. For example, the model takes the input data of given human faces and produces data that resembles the training dataset.

 

Refinement and Improvement

Once the output is produced, the Generative AI model undergoes a refinement process to enhance the output quality. The model may be fine-tuned by altering the parameters, refining the training process, or through techniques such as reinforcement learning to further improve the generated output.

 

Evaluation

The final stage is evaluation. Once the output is generated, its quality is checked using various metrics and assessment criteria. The model becomes successful when such generated output meets the desired criteria.

 

Thus, Generative AI uses cutting-edge AI techniques to understand the patterns of the given dataset and reproduce the same quality of output that resembles the originally fed datasets.

What are the Different Types of Generative AI Models?

Exploring the Diverse Generative AI Models!

To perform different functions perfectly, many techniques are used for Generative AI. As stated earlier, Generative AI uses neural networks, a type of architecture that functions and looks like a human brain. Deep learning also uses neural networks that contain more than three layers. So, let’s first understand the structure of neural networks.

Layers of interconnected nodes form a neural network where each node has an individual statistics model that processes a single facet of input based on its learning from the training data. It exactly functions like our brain where certain parts of the brain are dedicated to performing specific tasks. For instance, certain neurons might fire once we see a familiar face. In the same way, parts of neural networks also recognize different patterns and relationships based on the training data.

Individual nodes may recognize and provide individual decisions such as whether a word is a noun or not, the final output is caused by the domino effect when all these decisions come together as a complex output.

These neural networks are extensively used by data scientists to build a variety of Generative AI models. Let’s look at the most common architecture kinds.

 

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)

GAN and VAE are the two models used for image generation. The AI activity related to image generation started way back in the mid-2010s, thus giving us a glimpse of its capability in image generation in the present time. GANs function by one neural network generating an output, and the other one tries to analyze if it is fake or real. Through this kind of back-and-forth training, the model learns to create better images. Whereas the VAE generates encodings of the image parts and then creates sample parts of such encodings later decoding them to create new images.

 

Diffusion Models

In a diffusion model for generative AI, training data is destroyed via the addition of Gaussian noise and then recovered by reversing the addition of that noise. You can think of adding Gaussian noise as scrambling the pixels in the image. The noise is “Gaussian” because it’s added based on probabilities that lie along a bell curve. When the model reverses the noise, it makes predictions by decoding those probabilities, which results in an original image each time, even with the same prompt.

 

Large Language Models (LLMs)

With the advent of transformers, a new type of neural network, promising models of Generative AI such as LLM came into existence. Transformers can understand all the words of an input sentence in one go and find out how they are related, thus generating meaningful text. These transformers have been around since 2017 but training them with vast amounts of data and improving their performance helped to see a breakthrough in the form of today’s Generative AI natural language tools.

 

Hybrid models

In hybrid models, rule-based computation and machine learning along with neural networks are brought together to ensure human-like watchfulness of the AI system’s operations. Organizations can pick one of the above Generative AI models and attach rules-based or logic-based systems during or after their operations for performance enhancement or specific output.

Understanding the Depths of Generative AI and Other Components of AI

Knowing about the various use cases of Generative AI, we understand what AI can do and what it is up to in the future. Delving a little deeper and comprehending Generative AI and other systems of AI will help us see the AI world better.

 

Exploring and Understanding Machine Learning (ML) and Generative AI

Generative AI has been the most interesting development in AI. It is the sub-field of Machine Learning where a programmer is not required to give detailed instructions to the system to generate images, video, and music. The system learns through constant training with data sets and provides a similar output to the original one.

Traditional machine learning allows systems to recognize the patterns in data and predict the next step based on the training data. The main aim of Generative AI is to make the system independent and create new data based on the training dataset which is a completely new and almost human-like output. GANs, VAEs, and Autoregressive models are a few types of Generative AI that have sprung from the traditional machine learning approach. With constant progression in machine learning research, data scientists can find more sophisticated and realistic Generative AI models.

 

Exploring and Understanding Deep Learning and Generative AI

Deep learning, a subset of machine learning is engaged in training multiple layers of neural networks to identify patterns in data. Deep learning models such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNN) are used in Generative AI applications particularly to generate images and text. Deep learning allows the system to learn from enormous datasets including unstructured data which makes it stand out.

 

Exploring and Understanding Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Generative AI

Natural Language Processing (NLP) is a sub-field of AI that focuses on empowering systems to understand and interpret human language. Generative AI uses NLP techniques in the creation of product descriptions, news articles, text-to-speech applications, and more.

Recent developments in deep learning such as neural NLP models, RNNs, and transformer models, have boosted the performance of Generative AI applications based on NLP technologies. NLP is considered a powerful tool in Generative AI and by leveraging it, businesses can find new growth opportunities.

 

Exploring and Understanding Computer Vision and Generative AI

Computer Vision, as the name suggests, is that subfield of AI that enables systems to interpret and understand images and videos i.e. visual data. The combination of computer vision and Generative AI helps in creating new images and videos based on the training datasets.

In recent times, AI-generated realistic images that do not exist in the real world are being generated using these algorithms. The output of synthetic photographs can be utilized for various purposes in industries. Computer vision and Generative AI can be used to create synthetic photographs for the e-commerce industry, entertainment industry, medical sector, and many more.

 

Exploring and Understanding Predictive AI and Generative AI

Predictive AI and Generative AI are the two AI forces that are driving transformation in the technology itself which can bring more benefits in the future. Both technologies use machine learning algorithms; however, their goals are different. While Generative AI is focused on the creation of new content, Predictive AI focuses on providing the most accurate predictions.

Generative AI allows the system to learn from vast amounts of data and generate similar but new images, whereas predictive AI uses statistical analysis to find patterns in data to predict behavior and forecast events. However, both these technologies can be merged to maximize their benefits and apply them to different industrial sectors.

Frequently asked questions

What’s the difference between machine learning and artificial intelligence?
  • The difference between machine learning and artificial intelligence is that machine learning models use algorithms to recognize data patterns and apply those results to future analysis, whereas AI can analyze and contextualize data for human-like responses and actions.
What kinds of output can a generative AI model produce?
  • Generative AI can produce text, images, sounds, music, code, predictive data, and more.
Will generative AI eliminate human jobs?
  • While it’s near impossible to predict the future, generative AI shouldn’t eliminate jobs but rather enhance human skills and reduce repetitive tasks, making employees’ jobs easier.
Is generative AI reliable?
  • As with any type of data collection, generative AI’s reliability depends on the data source. It’s important to train generative AI models on accurate, reputable sources with verifiable data so the output contains trusted and correct information.
How can I use generative AI to improve the customer experience?
  • You can leverage generative AI to provide personalized support; create and update engaging self-service content in your knowledge base; gather insights from large datasets to improve your products and services; and automate processes that enhance support agent skills so they can provide exceptional customer service with every interaction.
